# Horror design review
Use the relevant [GAME_SPEC](../../../docs/GAME_SPEC.md), [DECISIONS](../../../docs/DECISIONS.md) and [OPEN_QUESTIONS](../../../docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md) entries to separate approved requirements, observed implementation and hypotheses. Read only the context needed for the current design question.

Start with the player's intended experience and the current decision. Ask only questions needed for that decision in accessible Japanese. Explain what players notice and what production effort changes. Use the available user-input tool; honor current delegation rather than repeating obsolete approval gates.

Examine both why a design might work and conditions that defeat it. When useful compare two or three concrete alternatives for uncertainty, agency, comprehension, pacing, repetition, fairness and audiovisual coherence. Do not assume darkness, enemies or more jump scares improve this game.

When a claim needs player evidence, use [PLAYTEST_PLAN](../../../docs/PLAYTEST_PLAN.md) for a small playable experiment: hypothesis, controlled difference, observable signals, human feedback and a keep/change/reject condition. GAME-015 authorizes prototypes within approved function and fairness; it does not establish adoption. Complete the requested comparison or evaluation preparation without turning every option into a new approval gate.

Keep internal rules precise while deciding how much information players receive. A camera sightline or audio trigger does not prove perception or fear. Use first-time play and post-play reflection; do not infer emotions solely from movement logs. AI agreement is critique, not player evidence.

Record decisions/reasons, rejected options that explain a tradeoff, open questions and the next useful evidence. Avoid speculative specifications and do not alter existing behavior to fit an invented answer.
